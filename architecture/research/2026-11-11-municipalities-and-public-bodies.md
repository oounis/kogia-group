# Municipalities and other public bodies: how they work and what an administrative platform can serve

Date: 2026-11-11
Scope: Libya first (accuracy priority), then Tunisia and the Gulf as reference and later markets.
Status of claims: every factual claim about a country's system carries a source link. Where a point could not be confirmed from a source read during this research it is marked **not verified**. Catalogues of processes, services and modules (A3, A4, B1, B2, B4) are design proposals by the author, built on the verified legal structure; they are not statements of law. Nothing here is legal advice.

Reading note on Libyan sources: most Libyan legal texts were read on two legal databases, the DCAF Libyan security sector legislation database (unofficial translations) and the Law Society of Libya legal compendium (Arabic text, unofficial transcription). Neither is the Official Gazette. Libya also has rival executive authorities; almost all decisions cited here were issued in Tripoli (GNA, then GNU). Whether a given municipality in the east or south applies them in practice is **not verified** and must be checked locally.

---

## Executive summary

1. **Libyan municipalities do not, in practice, issue birth certificates.** Civil status records (birth, death, marriage, divorce, family record, national number) belong to the Civil Registry Authority (maslahat al-ahwal al-madaniya), which runs a civil registry office in each municipality's territory. The confusion is understandable: the local administration law and its executive regulation list "civil registry" among municipal functions, but the unified municipal organisation chart adopted in 2021/2022 contains no civil registry division at all. See A1.6.
2. **What a Libyan municipality really issues**: commercial, industrial and craft activity licences; building, demolition, alteration, maintenance and excavation permits; local fee receipts; contractor registration certificates; decisions (removal orders); and, through the mukhtar al-mahalla, residence attestations and five other attestations. See A1.4 and A1.5.
3. **The legal door for electronic documents is open**: Law 6 of 2022 on electronic transactions, and Cabinet Decision 705 of 2024 creating the national "Dawlati" platform, which gives documents issued through it the same legal force as paper and orders all bodies to be able to verify them by QR code. A municipal platform should be designed to plug into that, not compete with it. See A1.8.
4. **Procurement news**: the Administrative Contracts Regulation 563 of 2007 was repealed and replaced by Cabinet Decision 600 of 2024 (Tripoli government). Any tender strategy must use the 2024 text. See B3.
5. **The best product wedge** is the internal side (correspondence and archive, licences office workflow, local revenue, complaints/1415 desk, council decisions) plus a citizen portal limited to what the municipality legally owns, with a routing "single window" for civil registry matters.

Key provisions of Law 59 (all from the [English text](https://security-legislation.ly/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/62-Law-No.59-of-2012_EN-1.pdf)):

- Art. 4: municipalities and their branches are created by Cabinet decree on the Minister's proposal; mahallat by ministerial decree on the governor's proposal.
- Art. 6: local units establish and manage public utilities within their scope, under the general directives of the Ministry of Local Government, and assume the functions entrusted to ministries by law, except national or special utilities determined by Cabinet decree.
- Art. 24 to 25: the municipality is the executive unit "which aims to provide services directly to citizens". General functions: urban planning, organisation, buildings, health and social affairs, water utilities, lighting, sanitation, roads, squares, bridges, local transport, public hygiene, gardens, recreation areas, shelters, real estate, public markets, construction permits for tourism and investment projects. Listed specific functions: (a) civil registry affairs, (b) regulation of municipal guard, local markets and slaughterhouses, (c) local roads and bridges, (d) issuing local permits, (e) monitoring environment and public health, (f) small business incubators.
- Art. 26: council elected by direct secret ballot, with at least one seat for women and one for a person with special needs from among the revolutionaries; the mayor is elected by the council members from among themselves (Art. 26, 33). Whether later election regulations changed the way the mayor is chosen is **not verified**.
- Art. 27: council powers (12 items): oversee administration, propose municipal regulations, propose and implement the budget, approve the final account, develop municipal resources, propose and collect local fees, follow development projects, support mahallat.
- Art. 28: a consultative Shura council of experts, half the size of the council, no vote.
- Art. 29 to 31: quorum is a simple majority; decisions by absolute majority of members present; minutes follow a fixed order; decisions must be published within one month. New agenda items can be forced by a civil society petition, or a petition by 2 percent (or 500) of citizens.
- Art. 33 to 35: the mayor is the chief executive and holds the Minister's financial and administrative powers for municipal services; the municipal bureau (diwan) executes decisions under the deputy (wakil).
- Art. 36 to 38: municipal branches are attached to the municipal bureau, deliver services to the mahallat in their area, and are run by a director appointed by the council.
- Art. 39 to 41: each mahalla has a mukhtar, appointed by the governor on the mayor's proposal; Libyan, resident, at least 40 years old; full time. Functions: notices, settlement of local disputes, reconciliation in civil and personal status disputes, participation in civil defence.
- Art. 42 to 43: High Council of Local Administration chaired by the Minister with governors and mayors.
- Art. 51: municipal financial resources (16 items): fees for municipal services; rents of leased municipal property; revenue of fairs, libraries, stadiums; proceeds of museums, gardens, clubs; share of taxes and customs allocated to the governorate; investment yield; 50 percent of sale value of buildings and properties sold by the municipality; fines and reconciliation settlements; advertising sales; proceeds of open markets, slaughterhouses, baths and municipal public transport; sales of goods confiscated by the municipal guard; government subsidies.
- Art. 53: collection of municipal fees and rents follows the rules on State funds.

Governorates: Law 59 places governorates above municipalities, and several powers (appointing mukhtars, exam committees) sit with the governor. Whether governorates are operating today, and who exercises the governor's powers where they are not, is **not verified**; the UNDP diagnostic describes the reform as unfinished ([UNDP rapid diagnostic of local governance](https://info.undp.org/docs/pdc/Documents/LBY/UNDP%20Libya%20Rapid%20Diagnostic%20of%20Local%20Governance%20-%20Synthesis%20Report%20(Final%20Version).pdf), not read in full).

### A1.2 Number of municipalities and elections

- A total of **143 municipalities** is the figure used in reporting on the 2024 to 2025 municipal elections (first phase covered 58 of 143) ([Anadolu Agency](https://aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/libya-concludes-municipal-elections-vote-counting-underway/3661374)). Other counts circulate ("more than 150" quoted by a law professor in [Libyan Express](https://www.libyanexpress.com/?p=61036)). An official Ministry of Local Government count was **not verified**; the number changes because municipalities are created by Cabinet decree.
- Elections are run by the High National Elections Commission. UNDP states council elections were held between November 2024 and February 2026 and lists 119 municipalities with completed elections ([UNDP Libya elections project](https://www.undp.org/libya/projects/promoting-elections-people-libya-0)). Phase 1 covered 58 municipalities and 426 seats ([UNSMIL](https://unsmil.unmissions.org/en/node/56361)); in phase 2 elections were suspended in a number of eastern and southern municipalities ([Libya Security Monitor](https://libyasecuritymonitor.com/eastern-authorities-suspend-municipal-elections-in-east-and-south/)).
- Product consequence: many councils are newly elected (2024 to 2026) with four-year terms (Law 59 Art. 9), which is a natural moment to propose new systems.

### A1.3 The Ministry of Local Government and its programmes

- The Ministry supervises municipalities, issues the unified structure and the licensing regulations cited above, and distributes transferred budget lines (Decision 856 of 2022 Art. 3: the Ministry of Finance transfers salaries and operating allocations of transferred staff to the Ministry of Local Government, which distributes them) ([Decision 856 of 2022](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=12784)).
- Decision 856 of 2022 enables municipalities to exercise local competences in: housing and construction, transport, economy and trade, planning, local affairs, industry, tourism, primary health care and public health, youth, culture, local radio. **Civil registry is not in that list.** ([Decision 856 of 2022](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=12784))

Observations that matter:

- There is **no civil registry department, section or unit** anywhere in the unified structure. The only reference to civil status is the 1968 civil status law in the preamble.
- The **Municipal Guard is not a municipal department**. The structure refers to it throughout as an external partner: "the Municipal Guard branch in the municipality", "in coordination with the Municipal Guard apparatus (jihaz)". It is a separate apparatus with its own law (Law 1 of 2016) and a regulation adopted by the Minister of Local Government (Decision 56 of 2019). Its precise chain of command (ministry versus mayor) is **not verified**. The Executive Regulation of Law 59 says a municipal guard is established in each municipality, protects consumers and economic operators, and its members have law-enforcement powers ([Executive Regulation Art. 78](https://security-legislation.ly/ar/?p=18494)). Goods it confiscates are a municipal revenue (Law 59 Art. 51).
- The structure itself mandates electronic systems in at least five places (archiving, licences reception, licences issuance, contractor register, complaints archive). That is a ready-made justification for a purchase.

### A1.5 Municipal branches, mahallat and the mukhtar

- **Municipal branch** (far' baladi): attached to the municipal bureau; delivers municipal and utility services to its mahallat; director appointed by the council (Law 59 Art. 36 to 38).
- **Mahalla**: smallest unit; one mukhtar each.
- **Mukhtar al-mahalla**, per the 2014 regulation ([Decision 159 of 2014](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=12006)): linked directly to the municipal branch or the mayor; conditions include Libyan nationality, actual residence in the mahalla, good conduct, literacy, age 40 or more, passing an oral and written exam; appointed for four years, renewable. Duties: execute instructions of the competent administrative bodies; **issue identification certificates, "ilm wa khabar" certificates and good-conduct certificates**; exercise the duties given to the mukhtar by the civil status, civil procedure and criminal procedure laws; report crimes; provide data and statistics; contribute to dispute settlement; take part in civil defence. He may not issue a certificate except on confirmed information and may make field visits to verify. He keeps a numbered, signed daily register ("sijill al-ahwal") issued by the municipal branch and shown to the mayor monthly.
- The 2020 amendment (to the 2018 regulation that replaced the 2014 one) lists the services precisely, each on an attached model form: **(1) ilm wa khabar of residence, (2) good conduct, (3) non-ownership of a dwelling, (4) not married to a non-Libyan, (5) ilm wa khabar about an event, (6) administrative statement relating to dispute settlement** ([Decision 43 of 2020](https://lawsociety.ly/legislation/%d9%82%d8%b1%d8%a7%d8%b1-%d8%b1%d9%82%d9%85-43-%d9%84%d8%b3%d9%86%d8%a9-2020-%d9%85-%d8%a8%d8%aa%d8%b9%d8%af%d9%8a%d9%84-%d8%ad%d9%83%d9%85-%d8%a8%d9%82%d8%b1%d8%a7%d8%b1-%d9%88%d8%b2%d9%8a%d8%b1/)). The full text of Decision 201 of 2018 and any fee for these attestations are **not verified**.
- Under the 1968 civil status law the mukhtar is also a fallback reporting channel: where there is no civil registry office, births and deaths are notified to the mukhtar or tribal chief, who forwards them to the registry ([Law 36 of 1968](https://security-legislation.ly/?p=24881)).

Product consequence: a "mukhtar digital desk" is legally coherent. The six attestations are defined, on fixed forms, with a duty to verify and a duty to keep a register. That is exactly a small workflow plus a numbered register plus a verifiable output.

### A1.6 Who issues which document in Libya

**The founder's assumption (municipalities issue birth certificates) is not correct as a description of practice today, although the mistake has a real legal root.**

The legal root: Law 59 Art. 25(a) lists "civil registry affairs" among municipal functions ([Law 59](https://security-legislation.ly/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/62-Law-No.59-of-2012_EN-1.pdf)); the Executive Regulation Art. 9 lists "civil registry" among facilities municipalities establish and manage, and Art. 80 says "a civil registry office is established in every municipality" to record births, residence, marriage, divorce and death, issue certificates and extract vital statistics ([Executive Regulation](https://security-legislation.ly/ar/?p=18494)). The 1968 civil status law also says a civil registry office is established "in every municipality" ([Law 36 of 1968 Art. 2](https://security-legislation.ly/?p=24881)).

The practice: those offices are offices of the **Civil Registry Authority (CRA, maslahat al-ahwal al-madaniya)**, a national body, not divisions of the elected municipality:

- The national number law states the national database is formed "in cooperation with the Civil Status Authority and the National ID project" and that national numbers are granted at birth "based on an individual's data in the Civil Registry at the Civil Status Authority" ([Law 8 of 2014](https://security-legislation.ly/?p=20489)).
- A Canadian IRB country-of-origin response describes the CRA as the body that issues birth, death, marriage, divorce and widowhood certificates, the family book and "civil status form 13", with about 400 offices, one in each municipality, main office in Tripoli; an IOM representative described it as administratively and financially independent and "technically" under the Ministry of Interior (the IRB could not corroborate the last point) ([IRB response](https://irb-cisr.gc.ca/en/country-information/rir/Pages/index.aspx?doc=456823)).
- The UK HM Passport Office profile: births are registered at the Civil Registry within 10 days and added to the family book; separate birth certificates are requested from the registry ([UK knowledge base profile](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/libya-knowledge-base-profile/libya-knowledge-base-profile)).
- Press reporting consistently treats the CRA as an Interior Ministry body (for example the Interior Ministry forming a committee on fake ID numbers: [Libya Observer](https://www.libyaobserver.ly/inbrief/interior-ministry-forms-committee-investigate-fake-id-numbers)). A primary decree fixing the CRA's affiliation to the Ministry of Interior was **not verified** in this research.
- The transfer-of-competences decisions of 2022 do not include civil registry ([Decision 856 of 2022](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=12784)), and the unified municipal structure has no civil registry unit ([Decision 1500 of 2022](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=36475)).

So: the civil registry office sits geographically in the municipality and carries the municipality's name, which is why citizens say "I got it from the baladiya", but the registrar, the register, the forms and the database are the CRA's.

**Commercial licence procedure as written** ([Decision 224 of 2018](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=10154)): application on form 1 to the municipal licences office with: tax registration certificate; 3 photos (individual activity); national number statement and ID copy; recent commercial registry extract (companies); title deed or lease of the premises; sketch of the premises certified by an approved engineering office (industrial and service activities); health certificate where required; qualification, sector approval and professional syndicate registration where required; environmental sanitation approval and impact study for activities with environmental impact. Applicant must be Libyan and 18 or older. The request is entered in a register and a receipt with the registration number is given. The office issues the licence **within 7 working days** after payment of fees. Validity: 1 to 3 years for individual, family and itinerant activities; 1 to 10 years for companies. The office keeps **a paper register and an electronic system**, with every change recorded (renewal, change of activity, name, cancellation), numbered and stamped pages, and monthly statistics. Renewal within the 3 months before expiry, with proof of social security contributions and chamber of commerce registration. Refusal can be appealed to a municipal grievance committee (chaired by the head of the economic development committee, with a Tax Authority delegate, the economic affairs director and a legal office member), which decides within 15 days. The licence must be displayed and shown to health inspectors and Municipal Guard officers. Fee amounts are **not verified** (not in the text read).

**Building permit procedure as written** ([Decision 225 of 2018](https://lawsociety.ly/legislation/%d9%82%d8%b1%d8%a7%d8%b1-%d8%b1%d9%82%d9%85-225-%d9%84%d8%b3%d9%86%d8%a9-2018-%d9%85-%d8%a8%d8%b4%d8%a3%d9%86-%d9%84%d8%a7%d8%a6%d8%ad%d8%a9-%d8%aa%d8%b1%d8%a7%d8%ae%d9%8a%d8%b5-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%a8/)): application to the licences office with real-estate certificate and cadastral map, approved technical prescription, three sets of architectural drawings signed by owner and designer (from a licensed engineering office). Large and investment projects: review by the local utilities and public works department and the urban planning and public property office within two weeks, then the municipal architectural committee (engineers appointed by the mayor), stamped drawings returned to the licences office. Small projects: field visit and technical report by the urban planning office. The licences office issues the permit and enters it in a serially numbered register **within 3 working days** after fees. First-degree permits are valid one year. The owner or supervising engineering office must notify the urban planning office at construction stages (setting-out line and periodic inspections are a condition to continue building). Second-degree permits (alterations, maintenance) need a technical condition report from an engineering office.

### A1.7 What this means for the "single window"

The municipality cannot issue civil status documents, but its citizen service office is legally tasked with receiving citizens and "directing them to the competent units" ([Decision 1500 of 2022](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=36475)). A portal can therefore honestly offer for civil registry, court and passport matters: (a) an information page with the correct body, documents and office; (b) a pre-filled request form the citizen prints or forwards; (c) where a local cooperation agreement exists with the civil registry office, an appointment or a forwarded request with tracking. It must never display "issue birth certificate" as a municipal service.

**How a single window can still route these requests honestly.** Three levels, to be agreed per municipality: Level 0 information (always possible). Level 1 "prepared file": the citizen fills the form once; the portal produces the exact official form pre-filled plus a checklist and an appointment suggestion, to hand in at the competent office. Level 2 "forwarded request": under a written cooperation protocol between the municipality and the local civil registry office (or court, or another body), the citizen service office transmits the file through a shared inbox, and the citizen tracks it; the issuing body still signs and issues. Level 2 needs the other body's consent and is a relationship project, not a software feature.

### What makes an electronically issued document acceptable (concepts, not legal advice)

1. **Authority of the issuer comes first.** A document is official because the competent officer issued it within his competence and entered it in the statutory register (the licences office register, the mukhtar's numbered register, the civil registrar's record). Technology proves that fact; it does not create it. So every electronic output must map to a register entry with a serial number, date and named responsible officer.
2. **Electronic signature** identifies a natural person (the mayor, the licences office director, the mukhtar) and shows his approval. Libyan Law 6 of 2022 sets validity conditions: unique to the signer, identifies him, under his sole control, and any later change is detectable ([Law 6 of 2022](https://lawsociety.ly/en/legislation/law-no-6-of-2022-concerning-electronic-transactions/?print=1), via summaries; full text not read). Whether a signature must come from a licensed certification provider to have full evidentiary weight is **not verified** and is the key question to put to a Libyan lawyer.
3. **Electronic seal** identifies the organisation (the municipality) and guarantees origin and integrity of bulk outputs such as receipts and licences. Tunisia gave explicit legal standing to a "visible electronic seal" before issuing birth extracts online ([Idaraty](https://idaraty.tn/fr/procedures/e-houwiya-extrait-naissance)). Whether Libyan law names the seal as a distinct concept is **not verified**.
4. **Verification channel.** A QR code and short code leading to an official verification page on a government domain lets any receiving body check the document against the live register. Libya's Dawlati decision makes QR verification the national expectation and obliges bodies to be able to check it ([Decision 705 of 2024](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=180895)). The QR is a pointer to the truth held in the register, not a signature in itself; a copied QR on a forged page fails as soon as someone scans it and compares.
5. **Integrity and time.** The PDF should be cryptographically signed or at least hashed, with the hash stored in the register, and time-stamped. A revoked or expired document must show as such on the verification page.
6. **Acceptance by the relying party.** The strongest practical lever is a circular from the Ministry of Local Government (or use of Dawlati) telling other bodies to accept the output. Until then, keep the **paper fallback**: the same document printed, wet-signed and stamped on request, carrying the same QR.
7. **Evidence file.** Keep who requested, how identity was checked, which officer approved, when, from which device; this audit trail is what a court or the audit bureau will ask for.

Identity verification options for citizens, weakest to strongest: phone number with one-time code; national number plus family record number matched against data the municipality already holds; first-time in-person activation at the citizen service office or by the mukhtar; future national digital identity or Dawlati login (**availability not verified**). Recommended: allow low-risk services (reports, tracking, payments, appointments) with phone OTP, and require one in-person activation for services that produce an attestation or licence.

## B3. How governments buy software in Libya and the region

### Libya: the administrative contracts regulation

**Correction to the brief.** General People's Committee Decision **563 of 2007** issuing the Administrative Contracts Regulation has been **repealed and replaced by Cabinet Decision 600 of 2024** (Government of National Unity, 31 October 2024), which expressly cancels the 2007 decision ([Decision 600 of 2024](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=86496); old text: [Decision 563 of 2007](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=7242)). Which regulation an entity in eastern Libya applies is **not verified**. A separate "organisational regulation for government procurement" was issued by Decision 12 of 2023 ([listing](https://lawsociety.ly/legislation/%d9%82%d8%b1%d8%a7%d8%b1-%d8%b1%d9%82%d9%85-12-%d9%84%d8%b3%d9%86%d8%a9-2023-%d9%85-%d8%a8%d8%a5%d8%b5%d8%af%d8%a7%d8%b1-%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%84%d8%a7%d8%a6%d8%ad%d8%a9-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%aa%d9%86%d8%b8%d9%8a/?print=1)); its content and relation to Decision 600 are **not verified**. Tender committees were organised by [Cabinet Decision 492 of 2013](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=5469) (current status **not verified**).

What the 2024 regulation says (read from the Arabic text at the link above; article numbers as shown there):

- **Contracting methods (Art. 8)**: (a) public tender, domestic or international; (b) limited tender among qualified executors registered in the entity's registers; (c) negotiation (mumarasa) by direct contact with registered specialists; (d) direct agreement; (e) public auction, for leasing or selling; (f) two-stage tender (technical proposals first, then financial offers from technically accepted bidders); (g) framework agreement at fixed prices for a period; (h) competition (for studies, designs).
- **General rule (Art. 9)**: works, supply and other administrative contracts are concluded by **public tender**; departure requires a ground in the regulation. Limited tender or negotiation is allowed for **consultancy services**, for works or supplies available only from specific executors, and other justified cases; administrative units need prior authorisation and must invite **at least three** executors.
- **Direct agreement (Art. 11)** is allowed, among others, for national security, emergency or urgent cases not caused by the entity; contracts with international organisations or foreign government bodies under agreements in force; when no one bids (with prior Cabinet approval); works or services required **on a trial basis**; transport and **simple purchases and works**; advertising and subscriptions; **technical, cultural, training or creative services**; and monopolised materials or spare parts, **consultancy services**, urgent maintenance and necessity purchases **up to 1,000,000 LYD**; above that and up to **5,000,000 LYD** prior authorisation of the Prime Minister is required; above that, Cabinet authorisation.
- **Before any procedure (Art. 12)**: precise definition of the subject, technical study and specifications, confirmation that the project is in the development plan or budget with sufficient allocation, legal conditions, and a statement from the Ministry of Planning that the project is not contracted twice. Two authorisations exist: authorisation to start contracting procedures, and authorisation to contract.
- **Bidder conditions (Art. 19)**: valid legal documents (commercial registry, activity licences and others); name **registered and classified** with the competent body; not debarred; not convicted in matters of honesty. The Cabinet sets the rules for registering and classifying executors and for the **blacklist** (Art. 17). Debarment cases are listed in Art. 20.
- **Guarantees**: the text refers to a preliminary deposit (ta'min ibtida'i) with the bid and a final deposit before signature; the percentages are **not verified**.
- **Taxes**: the contractor bears stamp duty and income taxes; they cannot be shifted to the contracting entity.
- **Standard contract forms** are mandatory; entities may not depart from them.
- **Platform**: the regulation defines "the platform" as a unified electronic portal for announcing government contracts (operation **not verified**).
- **Boycott undertaking**: every contract must include an undertaking to respect boycott legislation (Art. 18).

For comparison, the repealed 2007 text allowed direct assignment of consultancy services up to 1,000,000 LYD and required a higher approval above 2,000,000 LYD for certain direct assignments ([Decision 563 of 2007](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=7242)); other thresholds of the 2007 text were not extracted and are **not verified**.

Practical reading for a software vendor (not legal advice): software with implementation is likely framed as a service or consultancy-type contract, where limited tender, negotiation among at least three registered firms, a trial-basis direct agreement, or a direct agreement within the stated value limits are all conceivable routes. The buyer's legal office decides the route; the vendor's job is to be **registered and classified** beforehand and to make a trial or pilot easy to justify.

### Vendor registration and typical bidder documents (Libya)

Verified in texts read: valid commercial registry extract and activity licence; registration and classification with the competent body ([Decision 600 of 2024](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=86496)); municipalities keep their own register of contractors and suppliers and issue registration certificates ([Decision 1500 of 2022](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=36475)); activity licences require tax registration, and renewals require proof of social security contributions and chamber of commerce registration ([Decision 224 of 2018](https://lawsociety.ly/?p=10154)).

Typical bid file in practice (regional norm; exact Libyan list per tender, **not verified**): company registration extract and articles; valid activity licence; chamber of commerce certificate; tax clearance; social security clearance; bid bond or certified cheque (preliminary deposit); signed and stamped tender conditions; technical offer (method, work plan, team CVs, similar references with completion certificates); financial offer in a separate envelope; bank statement or financial statements; authorisation of the signatory; later a performance guarantee (final deposit) and stamp duty on the contract.

### Tunisia and the Gulf (orientation only, **not verified** in this research)

- Tunisia: public procurement is regulated by decree and runs through a national e-procurement system; expect registration on that platform, tax and social security attestations, and price-based evaluation with thresholds for simplified procedures. Verify current decree and thresholds before bidding.
- Gulf states: central e-procurement portals, supplier pre-registration and classification, local-presence or local-content requirements, mandatory use of national identity, payment and cloud-hosting frameworks, and formal cybersecurity compliance. Entry is realistically through a local partner or as a subcontractor.

## B4. What makes a vendor credible to a public buyer

| Factor | What the buyer wants | Can a small company honestly claim it early? |
|---|---|---|
| **On-premises or sovereign hosting** | Data stays in the country, ideally on the entity's own server or a national data centre | **Yes.** Offer on-premises and in-country hosting from day one; document the architecture and backup |
| **Data ownership clause** | All data belongs to the entity; export at any time in open formats; deletion on exit | **Yes.** Put it in the standard contract |
| **Source-code escrow** | Access to source if the vendor disappears | **Yes**, with a simple escrow deposit agreement with a bank, notary or law firm; state release conditions |
| **Audit trail** | Who did what, when, from where; tamper-evident | **Yes**, it is a product feature; demonstrate it |
| **Arabic-first interface** (plus French and English) | Real right-to-left design, Arabic search, Hijri and Gregorian dates, official letter formats | **Yes**, and it is a differentiator against imported products |
| **Documentation** | User manuals in Arabic, administrator guide, technical documentation | **Yes**, needs discipline rather than size |
| **Training** | Role-based training, train-the-trainer, certificates of attendance | **Yes** |
| **Maintenance SLA** | Response and resolution times, named contacts, on-site support | **Yes, but modestly**: promise only what the team can staff (for example business-hours support with defined response times); do not promise round-the-clock coverage early |
| **Security testing** | Independent penetration test, secure development practices, vulnerability handling | **Partly.** Commission one independent penetration test per major release and share the summary; follow OWASP guidance. Do not claim certification you do not hold |
| **References** | Similar deployments with contact persons and completion certificates | **Not at first.** Build them through a free or low-cost pilot with a written reference letter, a chamber of commerce or professional order, or a donor-funded pilot |
| **ISO 27001** (information security management) | Often requested by banks, Gulf entities and large SOEs | **Not early.** Honest wording: "security policies aligned with ISO 27001 controls; certification planned". Certification needs an audited management system and budget; plan it before approaching banks |
| **ISO 9001** (quality management) | Common in tenders as a tie-breaker | Achievable in the medium term for a small firm; claim only once certified |
| **ISO 22301** (business continuity) | Requested by critical operators | **Not early.** Provide instead a written backup and disaster-recovery plan with tested restore |
| **Financial and legal standing** | Registration, tax and social security clearances, bank guarantee capacity | **Yes**, keep all certificates current; guarantee capacity limits the tender size you can enter |
| **Local presence** | Office and staff in the country, classification in the supplier register | Required in practice; plan a Libyan legal entity or partner (legal form **not verified**) |
| **Interoperability and exit** | Standard database, documented APIs, ability to feed the national platform | **Yes**; state readiness to expose services to Dawlati and to consume a national identity or payment service when available |
| **Continuity of the team** | Named project manager, knowledge not held by one person | Partly; mitigate with documentation and escrow |

Honest early positioning in one sentence: a local, Arabic-first vendor offering on-premises deployment, full data ownership, escrow, a visible audit trail and a tested backup plan, with one or two documented pilot references and an independent penetration test summary, and with ISO certification on a dated roadmap rather than on the brochure.
