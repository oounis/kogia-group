## Per division

| Division | Primary model | Who pays | Distance to revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business** | SaaS per office/seat | Business owner | ⚡ **Shortest** |
| **Education** | B2B SaaS + subscription | Schools, parents, teachers | Medium — products exist |
| **Skills** | Employer subscription + premium | Companies, workers | Long — nothing built |
| **Play** | Purchase, subscription | Consumers | Longest |
| **Research** | Leads + B2B reports | Indirect | Indirect, but fastest to start |

## Why Business earns first

Its customer already runs a business, already feels the cost of the problem in
hours and money, and already pays for tools. There is no audience to build first.

Education is second because the products already exist and have users — what is
missing is the paying customer.

Skills is the largest long-term opportunity and the slowest to start: it needs
supply and demand at once.

## The funnel

```
Kogia Research → free indices and articles → search, social, press
        ▼
   kogiagroup.com — the ecosystem door
        ▼
Kogia Kids (free printables) ─► parents and teachers
        ▼
Paid products
```

**Kogia Kids is the top of the funnel for Education.** Free printables bring the
audience; Teacher Toolkit, Learning Passport and the school products sit behind them.

## Rules

1. **Every business unit needs a stated path to revenue.** Without one it is an
   experiment, and it is called one.
2. **Free is a strategy, not an accident.** Kogia Kids is free on purpose, to build
   the audience. That must be a decision with a date on it, not a default.
3. **Know the unit economics before scaling.** Especially anything using AI — cost
   per user must be known before the feature ships.
4. **Revenue reinvests into products**, not into new brands.

## Honest position, 2026-09-12

Kogia has **four live products and no meaningful revenue.** Everything above is a
plan, not a result.

The first real milestone is not a new division. It is **one stranger paying for
one thing.**
