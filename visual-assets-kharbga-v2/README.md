# Kharbga V3.2 — Refined Game Ecosystem Review

Product-level visual candidate built from documented Kharbga rules and Tunisian living heritage. The expanded desktop screenshots supplied in `C:/Current LAB/Khar/` informed interface density, navigation hierarchy, player bars, profiles, history, ratings, awards, progression, post-game flow and advertising placement. All Kharbga artwork, rules, naming and game objects remain original: no copied logo, chess notation, ranked chess pieces, kings or queens.

The layouts section contains six full views: desktop live play, quick-play home, player profile, history/performance, post-game result and mobile live play. The visual system uses sharp graphite surfaces, warm desert boards, cream text, one restrained green action colour, tabular numeric styling for clocks and ratings, and explicit active/focus states. V3.2 removes generic emoji from progression UI, fills the navigation canvas with live context, introduces native sponsored treatment, increases secondary-text legibility and adds designed chat, notification, reconnecting, empty and restored states.

Advertising inventory includes 970×90, 300×600, 300×250, 728×90, 320×100 and native sponsored-card placements. Ads are kept outside the board, clocks and primary controls.

## Historical caution

The game is unquestionably rooted in North-African and broader Arab living tradition, but the exact appearance date is uncertain. The Tunisian National Heritage Institute records collective-memory links to the Bey era and Hilalian transmission. Claims of a precise 2,500-year age or direct Roman descent should not be presented as established fact without stronger evidence.

## Authentic design anchors

- 7×7 board / 49 houses
- 24 equal Soldiers per player
- central salty house left empty during placement
- two pieces placed per turn
- first movement enters the middle house
- adjacent orthogonal movement only
- custodial enclosure capture and mandatory continuation
- Defender’s protected-Soldier exchange request
- sand, soil, chalk and cement boards
- date pits, pebbles, brick fragments and reused bottle caps
- intergenerational community play
